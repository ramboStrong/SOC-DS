# Procedural data-generation protocol

## Scope

The SOC-DS corpus is a controlled procedural observation corpus for
camera-centric distance-stratified image-plane density estimation. It is
not a physical orbital simulation, and Blender world units must not be
interpreted as kilometres or absolute line-of-sight range.

The frozen corpus used in the formal experiments contains 400 sequences:
10 nominal population scales from 50 to 500 in increments of 50, with
40 sequences and 100 stored frames per scale.

## Scene and camera

- Blender version: 5.2.0 LTS
- Rendering operation: OpenGL viewport rendering
- Viewport shading: `SOLID`
- Image format: PNG
- Resolution: 640 x 360 pixels
- Camera model: perspective
- Camera position: `(0.0, 25.0, -140.0)`
- Camera Euler rotation: `(0 deg, 180 deg, 0 deg)`
- Camera viewing direction: approximately `+Z`
- Focal length: 50 mm
- Sensor size: 36 x 24 mm
- Approximate horizontal/vertical field of view: 39.60 / 26.99 degrees

A Sun light with energy 3.0 is used. The procedural images are intended
to define a controlled observation model rather than photometrically
calibrated telescope imagery.

## Target population and motion

Each sequence is generated by copying the same source mesh (`Sat_0`) to
the requested nominal population size. For every target:

1. A distance stratum is selected with equal probability.
2. Camera-axis depth is sampled uniformly within the selected interval.
3. The vertical coordinate is sampled uniformly from `[0.1, 50.0]`.
4. The object scale is sampled uniformly from `[0.1, 0.4]`.
5. The target begins at `x=-40.0` and moves linearly along the x-axis.
6. The y-coordinate and camera-axis depth remain fixed within a sequence.

The prescribed speed contains a scale-dependent factor. Consequently,
the corpus may contain simulation-specific scale-motion cues. Scale does
not determine the distance-stratum label.

## Distance strata

Camera-centric depth is expressed in Blender world units.

| Stratum | Sampling interval | Decision rule |
|---|---:|---|
| Near | `[140, 210]` | `depth < 220` |
| Middle | `[230, 310]` | `220 <= depth < 325` |
| Far | `[340, 460]` | `depth >= 325` |

The gaps between the sampling intervals and decision thresholds are a
property of the frozen corpus and are treated as a limitation rather
than as physical separation between orbital regimes.

## Visibility and frame metadata

A target contributes to supervision only when its projected centre is
inside the image and lies in front of the camera. For every target and
frame, the generator stores:

- world coordinates,
- camera-axis depth,
- Euclidean camera range,
- object scale,
- projected pixel coordinates,
- visibility,
- assigned and threshold-computed stratum labels.

The nominal population scale is therefore not necessarily equal to the
visible count in every frame.

## Star-field augmentation

A sequence-specific star field is added in image space. At 640 x 360,
the augmentation samples 120--260 stars; approximately 8% are assigned
to the brighter group. Normal-star intensities are sampled from
`[90, 180]`, bright-star intensities from `[180, 255]`, and radii from
1--3 pixels. Star positions are excluded within 5 pixels of visible
target projections collected over the sequence.

The augmentation additionally uses:

- Gaussian glow with standard deviations 0.8 and 1.8 pixels,
- mild frame-dependent twinkling with strength 0.08,
- additive Gaussian image noise with standard deviation 3.0,
- a background intensity offset sampled from `[0, 4]`.

The generated corpus and per-sequence metadata were frozen before the
formal multi-seed experiments. The released frozen corpus, rather than
regeneration under an arbitrary absolute file path, defines the
evaluated dataset.

## Density labels

Visible target coordinates are mapped from 640 x 360 to an 80 x 45
label grid. Each target contributes a truncated two-dimensional Gaussian
kernel to its assigned stratum:

- standard deviation: 1.5 grid cells,
- truncation radius: `3 sigma`,
- discrete kernel sum: 1.

The kernel is renormalized over the valid image support, including near
boundaries. Therefore, the integral of each target contribution remains
one. The three stratum maps are summed to obtain the total-density map.

## Camera-depth supervision

Camera-axis depth is normalized using the fixed corpus range `[140, 460]`
and clipped to `[0, 1]`. A target-supported depth map and soft validity
mask are generated on the same 80 x 45 grid with a Gaussian standard
deviation of 1.5 grid cells.

For overlapping target supports, the depth value is the Gaussian-weighted
average of the contributing normalized depths. The validity mask is the
accumulated Gaussian weight clipped to `[0, 1]`. Unlike the density
kernel, the depth-support kernel is not normalized to unit integral.

## Frozen split

For every population scale:

- sequences 1--30: training,
- sequences 31--35: validation,
- sequences 36--40: testing.

The first stored frame is used only as temporal context. Each 100-frame
sequence therefore contributes 99 two-frame samples:

| Split | Sequences | Stored frames | Two-frame samples |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Training | 300 | 30,000 | 29,700 |
| Validation | 50 | 5,000 | 4,950 |
| Test | 50 | 5,000 | 4,950 |

The split is performed at the sequence level; no frames from the same
sequence appear in multiple subsets.

## Seed manifest

`manifests/dataset_seed_manifest.csv` records the Blender sample seed and
star-enhancement seed for all 400 sequences. Absolute local paths have
been removed from the public manifest.
